OPERATIONS MANUAL

How the Atlas Harbor logistics game works

You run a logistics control tower by exception. Routine production and safe movement can be delegated. Your job is to intervene when a customer promise, route, capacity limit, or disruption creates a meaningful tradeoff.

Open the live network

Your first morning

The game starts Monday at 08:00 with three shipments already moving. A $420,000 holiday-toy contract has suffered a truck breakdown near Albuquerque with seven simulated hours remaining. You immediately choose whether to send a replacement truck, transfer critical cartons through the Memphis air hub, or wait for repair.

Each option changes cash, customer trust, on-time performance, or staff morale. There is no hidden “correct” answer: the decision depends on the promise, the customer’s tolerance, and what future flexibility you want to preserve.

The normal play loop

  1. Resolve the waiting exception. Review the contract value, time remaining, customer tolerance, and the stated consequences.
  2. Let routine work continue. Production and safe dispatches can be delegated through Team & automation.
  3. Use Next decision. The simulation advances through routine hours until another issue requires judgment.
  4. Inspect the map. Click contracts, facilities, vehicles, routes, and competitors to change a plan or understand a risk.
  5. Protect the portfolio. Decide which orders deserve expensive recovery and which risks can be accepted.
  6. Invest. Spend cash on Smart Dispatch, Recovery Desk, Relationship Team, and Network Visibility upgrades.
  7. Build the company. Deliveries and good decisions award experience and unlock later chapters.

Routes and movement

Truck routes follow road geometry supplied through Atlas Harbor’s routing API and OpenStreetMap-based OSRM. Rail movements use curated intermodal corridors, air movements use curved flight paths, and the ocean competitor follows a water corridor between ports.

A vehicle’s marker and percentage are calculated from the same path. The movement card shows two numbers:

  • Overall progress is distance completed across the whole multi-leg journey.
  • Current-leg progress is distance completed between the current pair of facilities.

At an intermediate stop, current-leg progress resets to zero while overall progress continues. The contract progress bar also includes production and readiness, so it is not intended to match the vehicle percentage exactly.

Time and ETA

The game advances in simulated hours. It also advances one simulated hour every 15 real minutes while the page remains open and unpaused. Transit time is based on routed distance and a compressed mode-specific game speed. It is internally consistent, but it is not a real carrier quotation.

The simulation does not advance while the page is closed. It resumes from the last saved hour.

Persistence

Every meaningful action saves immediately on the current device. Signed-in players also save their full career in Supabase account metadata, so no logistics-specific database table must be initialized.

The saved career includes contracts, inventory, routes, movement progress, exceptions, cash, trust, on-time service, staff morale, delegation, upgrades, experience, and simulation time. The header reports whether the game is saved locally, synced to the account, or waiting to retry.

Older table-backed saves are imported when the legacy game_progress table exists.

Traveling salesman clarification

A pure traveling-salesman problem finds the shortest tour through a collection of interchangeable stops. The standard game contract has an origin, an optional required processing stop, and a destination. Those stops cannot usually be reordered without breaking the process.

The more appropriate future mechanic is a capacitated vehicle-routing scenario: combine several compatible customer orders on a limited fleet while respecting deadlines, capacity, and service windows. The server already exposes a stop-order optimization endpoint for that expansion.

Team and automation

Team & automation lets you delegate routine dispatch, production, and updates. Delegation reduces clicking; it does not remove strategic responsibility. Unsafe or urgent conditions are escalated to the player.

Maya manages customer relationships, Diego coordinates transportation and reroutes, and Nia manages production and quality. Their morale changes after some decisions.

Decision support

Optimize current priorities produces a transparent weighted recommendation based on urgency, value, inventory readiness, and customer tolerance. It does not execute anything.

Signed-in players with an AI provider can ask the AI Operations Advisor to challenge a plan. The AI receives a compact simulation snapshot and can only recommend; it cannot change the game state.

Known limitations

  • Rail paths are approximate corridors, not exact track-level geometry.
  • Road routing depends on an external OSRM service; a less precise corridor fallback is used during outages.
  • Simulation time is compressed.
  • The game does not advance while closed.
  • Competitor share is a narrative score rather than a market model.
  • There is not yet a finite shared pool of tractors, drivers, trailers, or warehouse doors.
  • Orders cannot yet be consolidated onto one vehicle, so the full capacitated vehicle-routing problem is not implemented.
  • Staff morale does not yet cause absence or turnover.

Resetting

Reset my game requires confirmation and clears only the logistics career. It does not delete the account or work in other Problem Spaces.
