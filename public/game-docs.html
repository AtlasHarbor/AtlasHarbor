OPERATIONS MANUAL

Run the company. Dispatch the exceptions.

You are the General Manager and Lead Dispatcher of a growing global 3PL. Departments handle routine work inside policy. You manage people, fleet and contracted capacity, carrier relationships, cash, customer promises, and international growth—then step into dispatch when judgment matters.

Open the live network

Your first morning

The first chapter starts with a U.S. truck breakdown so you can learn recovery decisions quickly. That incident is only the opening scene: the Global 3PL Control Tower gives you carrier capacity and opportunities across China, South Korea, Japan, Indonesia, India, the UAE, Kenya, the Netherlands, the U.K., Australia, Colombia, and the United States.

Your role

  • General Manager: staffing, cash, policy, carrier relationships, fleet strategy, international expansion, and company trust.
  • Lead Dispatcher: difficult loads, driver coordination, exceptions, expedites, mode changes, threatened promises, and recovery.
  • 3PL network builder: decide what to own, what to contract, which partners to develop, and which lanes are worth pursuing.

The game is intentionally management by exception. If everything requires a click, the company is designed badly.

Departments

Click the green routine-operations indicator or Manage departments to open the company console.

  • Dispatch & Capacity — Diego Ramos: safe releases, capacity matching, driver availability, transport escalations.
  • Production Control — Nia Brooks: inventory readiness, packaging, quality, throughput, and handoffs.
  • Customer Operations — Maya Chen: routine updates, promises, relationship risk, and escalation.
  • Global Trade Desk — Amina Hassan: overseas partners, documents, customs readiness, and international handoffs.

You can change delegation, department headcount, and operating policy. The first three toggles directly control the underlying simulation automation.

Fleet & capacity

The fleet tab separates owned road equipment from contracted capacity. You can inspect owned tractors and trailers, contracted vessel/block-space and air allotments, move equipment into preventive maintenance, and add flexible capacity through carrier contracts.

Ownership gives control but creates maintenance exposure. Contracting protects flexibility but depends on provider reputation, rate, relationship, and availability.

Carrier network

Carriers are a relationship book rather than anonymous mode buttons. Each fictional provider has supported markets, service reputation, relationship score, relative rate, visible capacity, and a named contact. Calling a carrier can change the visible availability response; contracting capacity costs cash and adds an asset to your capacity inventory.

People & HR

Drivers have operating regions, endorsements, reliability, available hours, morale, and status. You can offer a priority load; the driver may accept or decline based partly on reliability and morale. HR also has a recruiting pipeline so you can add people in new markets.

Global network

The World Network tab includes Los Angeles/Long Beach, Savannah, Cartagena, Rotterdam, Felixstowe, Jebel Ali/Dubai, Nhava Sheva/Mumbai, Mombasa, Shanghai, Shenzhen/Yantian, Busan, Yokohama, Tanjung Priok/Jakarta, and Sydney/Port Botany.

Candidate opportunities span China → U.S., Korea → Netherlands, UAE → India, Kenya → U.K., Japan → Australia, Indonesia → UAE, Colombia → U.S., and China → Australia air recovery. Booking a lane consumes the buy cost immediately and creates an active international move. When enough game time passes, completed service posts revenue and trust.

The world-map arcs are commercial planning views, not literal AIS tracks or real carrier quotations. The operating map still follows the game’s mode and landmass constraints.

What does “Challenge lane” mean?

Game jargon now has an info icon. Click it for an explanation of the mechanic. Challenge lane means spending commercial effort and cash to compete for a customer corridor where a rival is strong. It is a sales/network action, not a physical reroute.

Other explained terms include Management by exception, Smart Dispatch, Recovery Desk, Network Visibility, Customer promise, Expedite, Air recovery, Intermodal rail, Priority handling, and Facility utilization. The management console also has a complete Game Terms tab.

The normal play loop

  1. Resolve the waiting exception.
  2. Open Departments and decide what the team can run without you.
  3. Review the fleet, contracted capacity, drivers, and carrier relationships.
  4. Use Next decision to move routine game time forward.
  5. Intervene when service, cost, risk, or customer trust requires judgment.
  6. Call carriers and recruit or coordinate drivers when capacity gets tight.
  7. Book international opportunities when the expected margin and service risk make sense.
  8. Invest in company capabilities and build a resilient network.

Routes and movement

Truck routes follow road geometry supplied through Atlas Harbor’s routing API and OpenStreetMap-based OSRM. Rail uses curated intermodal corridors, air uses flight arcs, and ocean operating routes use configured water corridors. Trucks and rail cannot cross landmasses; ocean must start and end at ports.

The active-chapter operating map and the global commercial planning map serve different purposes. The global view does not bypass routing rules.

Time and persistence

The simulation advances in game hours and also one simulated hour every 15 real minutes while open and unpaused. It does not advance while the page is closed.

Game progress is saved on the device and, for signed-in users, inside user_metadata.atlas_problem_spaces.logistics_game.progress. The new management state travels inside the same game object, including departments, fleet, carriers, drivers, recruiting, and global contracts.

Reality boundary

Customer, carrier, employee, competitor, rate, reputation, and opportunity data are fictional scenario data. Routing and geography are used to make the decisions coherent; this is not a live freight marketplace, labor system, carrier quotation, or customs system.
