THE LOGISTICS CONNECTION

A dinner decision uses the same structure as a network decision.

Origins and travel cost

The confirmed group location is the origin. Distance is the route burden, like moving freight from a plant to a customer.

Hard constraints

Hours, distance, price ceilings, dislikes, and dietary needs define which options are feasible.

Multiple stakeholders

Each person has a taste profile. The solver avoids maximizing one person’s happiness while making another person miserable.

Freshness and uncertainty

Map listings cannot prove delivery day, cold-chain handling, allergen controls, or raw-shellfish safety. Those become explicit verification tasks.

Objective function

Rankings combine minimum participant fit, average fit, travel and availability, evidence confidence, and freshness questions.

Decision, then execution

The output explains the compromise, shows who is least satisfied, and identifies what the group must call or verify before leaving.

Restaurant facts, menus, hours, dietary handling, harvest information, and availability can change quickly. Verify consequential dietary, allergy, and raw-shellfish questions directly with the restaurant. Atlas Harbor is decision support, not a food-safety certification.
