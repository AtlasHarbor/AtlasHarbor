THE LOGISTICS CONNECTION

A meal decision uses the same structure as a network decision.

Origin and route burden

The confirmed location is the origin. Distance is the travel, pickup, or delivery burden, like moving freight from a facility to a destination.

Hard constraints

Hours, meal time, service style, distance, price ceilings, dislikes, and dietary needs define which options are feasible.

One or many stakeholders

A solo decision maximizes one diner’s fit. A shared decision protects the least-satisfied person before improving the average.

Freshness and uncertainty

Map listings cannot prove delivery day, cold-chain handling, allergen controls, or raw-shellfish safety. Those become explicit verification tasks.

Objective function

Rankings combine diner fit, breakfast-to-late-night availability, service mode, travel, evidence confidence, and freshness questions.

Decision, then execution

The output explains the fit, exposes tradeoffs, and identifies what to call or verify before ordering or leaving.

Restaurant facts, menus, hours, service modes, dietary handling, harvest information, and availability can change quickly. Verify consequential dietary, allergy, delivery, takeaway, and raw-shellfish questions directly with the restaurant. Atlas Harbor is decision support, not a food-safety certification.
